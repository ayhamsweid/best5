## Search intent and editorial promise

Help a family decide between an Arabic-medium/Arabic-centred school and a British/Cambridge pathway in Istanbul. There is no universal winner. The answer changes with the child's current academic language, final qualification, university destination, Arabic and identity goals, mobility, legal eligibility and total cost.

“Arabic school” and “British school” must not be treated as single standard products:

- Arabic options may use a national Arab curriculum, an American programme taught in English, or a mixed multilingual model with strong Arabic.
- British options may be fully British, Cambridge through A Levels, Cambridge through IGCSE followed by IB, or a dual Turkish/Cambridge route.
- IGCSE is normally a 14–16 qualification and is not by itself the final university-entry qualification. Families must verify the post-IGCSE route.

## Best5 structure: five decision tests

1. Child's language and readiness
2. Final diploma and university destination
3. Arabic literacy, identity and religious provision
4. International portability and timing of a transfer
5. Total cost, commute and legal eligibility

These are decision tests, not five schools and not a claim that one system is universally superior.

## Evidence standards

- Use Cambridge and Pearson first-party qualification pages for qualification claims.
- Use BISI and MEF first-party pages only as examples of different British/Cambridge routes.
- Use Al Aqsa's first-party pages only as an example showing that one school can have separate Arabic and American divisions.
- Use the current MEB private-school directory, Law No. 5580 and e-Denklik for legal-status and equivalency checks.
- Do not publish unverified current fee figures.
- Tell readers to obtain the exact campus, legal school name, curriculum, awarding body, final diploma and a dated itemised quote in writing.
